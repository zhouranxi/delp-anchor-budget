\section{S3. Equivalence testing}
\noindent The main text reports several comparisons as ``no difference
detected'' or ``reproduces the base system''. A failure to reject is not
evidence of equivalence, so each such comparison is accompanied here by a
two-one-sided-tests (TOST) equivalence test at a pre-declared margin of
equivalence of 0.5 accuracy points, reported as a 90\% confidence
interval (the interval that corresponds to $\alpha=0.05$ in a TOST). A
comparison is declared equivalent only when the whole 90\% CI lies inside
$[-0.5,0.5]$; otherwise the honest statement is that a difference of
up to the CI half-width cannot be excluded at $n=10$.

\noindent Two of these results deserve to be stated plainly rather
than left to the reader. First, the granularity ladder's null is
\emph{not} uniformly supported by equivalence testing: the
branch-versus-neuron comparison on the learning term is equivalent at the
declared margin in the two MNIST cells but not in the Fashion cells and not
for final accuracy, so the correct summary is that we detected no
difference larger than roughly half a point in the cells where the effect
is small, not that the two constructions are interchangeable everywhere.
Second, the constant-gate control does not formally reproduce the base
system: its 90\% CI on the learning term is
$[-1.58,+0.92]$ on Fashion-MNIST and $[-0.43,+0.57]$ on MNIST, so an
effect of up to about one and a half points cannot be excluded on
Fashion-MNIST. The main text therefore describes the constant-gate arm as
being consistent with the base system rather than as reproducing it.
